# Electricity-price stories in Finnish media, Oct 2024 – Oct 2026
## A taxonomy of recurring meta topics (merged report, v2)

Scope: Finnish-language news media (plus some Swedish-language Finnish outlets), 21 October 2024 – 21 October 2026.

This version merges three research passes. Each source is used for what it does best:

- **Claude's research report**: the syndication mechanics, the wire-copy template and the supply-shock coverage.
- **ChatGPT's article index**: 397 date-confirmed articles from 30 outlets, coded to 18 story angles. All article counts below come from this index.
- **Gemini's analysis**: hybrid-contract coverage, grid regulation and the bill-structure argument. Figures that appear only in Gemini's version are marked *(unverified)*.

---

## TL;DR

- **A topic is the story angle a reader is offered, not the event that caused it.** One Olkiluoto outage or one cold snap produces an alert, an explainer, a household story, a contract-advice piece and a political argument. This report therefore uses **19 story angles in six families**. Causes (weather, nuclear, hydro, interconnectors, demand, geopolitics, market design) are tracked separately as **cause tags**.
- **The daily "Pörssisähkö on huomenna kallista" (spot power is expensive tomorrow) alert is the dominant format by volume.** It runs through at least three distribution networks: STT, Uutissuomalainen (the Keskisuomalainen group's shared newsroom) and Kauppalehti's own daily price series. Each network fans the same text out to many sites.
- **Below the alerts there are four larger content territories:** forecasts and contract timing, the household bill and its non-energy parts, everyday consequences (sauna, appliances, automation), and the structural fight over who pays for the future power system (data centres, investment economics, grid fees, fairness).
- **The coverage contains a paradox.** Alarm about spikes was intense even though Finland's annual spot average in 2024–2025 was among the lowest in Europe. Meanwhile the non-competitive part of the bill (grid fees and taxes, roughly 60% by Gemini's estimate) gets far less attention than tomorrow's auction, even though households cannot reduce it by timing their sauna.
- **The centre of gravity shifted over the two years.** Coverage moved from crisis aftermath and sabotage (late 2024), to cheap prices and market redesign (2025), to the expensive winter of January–February 2026, to the structural questions of autumn 2026: data centres, grid fees, Olkiluoto 3 timing, hydro deficit and party politics.

---

## 1. How to read this taxonomy

Every article gets **one primary angle** (what the reader is told or offered) and **any number of cause tags** (why the price moved). This separation matters for two reasons:

1. It stops recurring causes from appearing as separate topics. "Olkiluoto" isn't a story type. It's a trigger that feeds several story types.
2. It lets you cross-tabulate questions such as "which causes generate household stories versus policy stories", which is more useful for content planning than a flat list.

**A note on the counts.** Article counts come from ChatGPT's 397-article index. Treat them as a **relative ranking, not volume**:

- **Syndication is undercounted.** Only 31 of the 397 rows are flagged as possible shared stories, yet a single wire alert typically appears on 5–15 sites the same day.
- **There is a recency bias.** 35 of the 78 "expensive tomorrow" items fall in Q3 2026 and none in Q2 2025. The daily alert ran throughout, so this reflects search indexing, not publishing.
- **Paywalled outlets are coded mainly from headlines and leads.**

## 3. The angles in detail

### Family A — Daily price news

#### 1. "Pörssisähkö on huomenna kallista": the expensive-price alert
**Pattern.** A short item of 80–200 words giving tomorrow's VAT-inclusive daily average, the most expensive hour or quarter-hour, a one-line cause, and often a tip to avoid heating the sauna between 19 and 21. Same-day follow-ups ("tänään huippulukemissa", today at peak levels) and record stories ("vuoden kallein päivä", the most expensive day of the year) belong here too.

**How it is produced.** Nord Pool publishes day-ahead results in the early afternoon, Finnish time. Three distribution networks then turn them into copy:
- **STT** wire copy, republished by Yle Lyhyesti, MTV Uutiset, Maaseudun Tulevaisuus, Suomenmaa, SSS.fi and others, usually 16:00–22:00.
- **Uutissuomalainen**, the Keskisuomalainen group's shared newsroom, which runs the same headline across Keskisuomalainen, Etelä-Suomen Sanomat, Kouvolan Sanomat, Karjalainen and other papers.
- **Kauppalehti**, which runs its own daily price series ("Sähkön hinta pomppaa selvästi tiistaina", the price jumps sharply on Tuesday). Ilta-Sanomat/Taloussanomat and Verkkouutiset add their own versions on spike days.

Most of these stories cite consumer price-tracker sites rather than Nord Pool or Fingrid. Since 1 October 2025 the vocabulary has shifted from "tuntihinta" (hourly price) to "kallein varttitunti" (the most expensive quarter-hour).

**Headline formulas**
- "Pörssisähkö on huomenna kallista": *Spot is expensive tomorrow*
- "Pörssisähkö kallistuu [viikonpäivänä] rajusti – näitä tunteja kannattaa pitää silmällä": *Spot jumps sharply on [day] – watch these hours*
- "Pörssisähkö maksaa pian yli 50 senttiä kilowattitunnilta": *Spot will soon cost over 50 cents per kWh* (Verkkouutiset style)
- "Pörssisähkön hinta tänään huippulukemissa": *Spot at peak levels today*

**Examples**

- Maaseudun Tulevaisuus, 21 Sep 2026: "Pörssisähkö kallistuu tiistaina rajusti – näitä tunteja kannattaa pitää silmällä" – https://www.maaseuduntulevaisuus.fi/uutiset/5aeef461-6d07-40ed-8f82-11289638469e
- Keskisuomalainen (Uutissuomalainen), 8 Sep 2026: "Pörssisähkö on keskiviikkona kallista" – https://www.ksml.fi/uutissuomalainen/9776278
- Kauppalehti, 19 Oct 2026: "Sähkön hinta pomppaa selvästi tiistaina" – https://www.kauppalehti.fi/uutiset/a/cdc0e8cd-b6e4-4f4d-b9bb-5412d0289fa3
- Yle, 14 Oct 2025: "Pörssisähkö on illalla kallista – hintapiikkeihin on syytä varautua jatkossakin" – https://yle.fi/a/74-20188321

**Note.** Many outlets also run a neutral variant ("Sähkön hinta pysyy tavanomaisena sunnuntaina", the price stays normal on Sunday). The series is a fixed daily slot, not just an alarm.

### Family B — Outlook and explanation

#### 3. Scorecards and records
**Pattern.** "Last month's average was X c, the highest or lowest since Y". Annual roundups appear in early January, often built on Energiateollisuus statistics. This angle is thinly indexed in the corpus but very common on comparison and affiliate sites, which compete with the media for the same searches.

**Examples**
- Iltalehti, 5 Jan 2025: "Tämän verran sähkö maksoi Suomessa vuonna 2024" – https://www.iltalehti.fi/digiuutiset/a/60d3ede3-72e0-4e98-8609-056a9ef0a22c
- Ilta-Sanomat, 4 Jan 2025: "Vuosi sitten sähkö ampaisi kauhulukemiin – tämä on tilanne nyt" – https://www.is.fi/taloussanomat/art-2000010942893.html
- Ilta-Sanomat, 15 Apr 2026: "Näin halpaa pörssisähkö oli maaliskuussa" – https://www.is.fi/taloussanomat/art-2000011945167.html
- Verkkouutiset, Jan 2026: "Näin pörssisähkön hinta on kehittynyt: yksi kuukausi erottuu selvästi" – https://www.verkkouutiset.fi/a/nain-porssisahkon-hinta-on-kehittynyt-yksi-kuukausi-erottuu-selvasti/

#### 4. Seasonal and annual outlook
**Pattern.** "Ennuste: talvesta tulee halpa/kallis" (forecast: a cheap/expensive winter). Built on Nasdaq/EEX futures, Nordic hydro balance and expert quotes, usually ending with contract advice. The biggest wave came in August 2026, when HS, Kauppalehti, MTV, Yle, IS, IL and regional papers all ran "brace for an expensive autumn" stories within about two weeks.

**Examples**
- Yle, 10 Dec 2025: "Ennuste: pörssisähkö on halpaa tänä talvena – vertaa pörssisähkön ja kiinteän hinnan lukuja" – https://yle.fi/a/74-20198436
- Tekniikka & Talous, 26 Apr 2026: "Näin paljon sähkö maksaa Suomessa seuraavan 5 kk aikana" – https://www.tekniikkatalous.fi/uutiset/a/248d7ce7-f8ba-405e-ae5f-7e2d9fa50e2a
- Ilta-Sanomat, 28 May 2026: "Nyt tuli arvio: sähkön hintaan kova harppaus" – https://www.is.fi/taloussanomat/art-2000012034546.html
- Yle, 18 Aug 2026: "Sähkö kallistuu syksyllä – asiantuntijat neuvovat, miten hintojen nousuun voi varautua" – https://yle.fi/a/74-20241542
- MTV Uutiset, 20 Aug 2026: "Näin sähkön hinta nousee – helpotusta luvassa vasta keväällä" – https://www.mtvuutiset.fi/artikkeli/nain-sahkon-hinta-nousee-helpotusta-luvassa-vasta-kevaalla/9380478

**The genre's weakness.** Futures are a hedging price that includes a risk premium. They are not a weather forecast, and the coverage repeatedly treats them as predictions. Yle's December 2025 "cheap winter" story (futures around 7 c) was followed by January–February 2026 averages of 14.72 and 17.22 c/kWh. In the other direction, Q3 2026 futures of around €54/MWh in May were followed by a July average of about €18/MWh *(Gemini; unverified)*. When high autumn futures are presented as a certain outcome, readers are nudged to lock in fixed prices at the peak of the risk premium.

### Family C — Contracts and consumer protection

#### 7. Spot or fixed: which actually saves money?
**Pattern.** Real or model households compared, with risk tolerance discussed. In 2024–2025 the verdict was usually "spot wins over time". After February 2026 the framing became more ambivalent: spot users were "in almost the worst possible position", yet few actually left.

**Examples**
- HS, 9 Feb 2026: "Kolmen todellisen kodin vertailu kertoo, kenelle pörssisähkö on paras vaihtoehto" – https://www.hs.fi/visio/art-2000011794089.html

#### 9. The hybrid contract that disappointed
**Pattern.** Consumption-impact contracts (kulutusvaikutussopimus, e.g. Fortum Duo, Helen Välkky, Oomi Jousto) are sold as a safe middle ground: a fixed base price plus or minus a monthly "consumption impact". Coverage explains why most homes end up paying more:
- Households naturally use the most power when prices are highest, so the impact tends to be positive (cost-increasing).
- Monthly settlement punishes cold spells in electrically heated homes.
- Cutting consumption evenly across hours doesn't change the impact at all.

The big wave came in summer 2024, just before this window. HS ran "Asiakkaat raivostuivat uudesta sähkösopimuksesta" (customers furious over a new contract), alongside pieces from Tekniikan Maailma and Yle. Gemini cites Oomi data showing 67% of hybrid customers had a positive impact averaging +1.15 c/kWh *(unverified)*. Inside the window the angle recurs mainly as a sub-theme of the "trap" and "spot or fixed" stories, e.g. Lapin Kansa, 25 Aug 2026: "Olen kuvaillut sitä laimennetuksi pörssisähkösopimukseksi" ("I've described it as a diluted spot contract") – https://www.lapinkansa.fi/olen-kuvaillut-sita-laimennetuksi-porssisahkosopim/13627290.

**Why keep it separate.** Hybrids are often among the cheapest-looking offers on comparison portals, so every switching season brings this angle back.

#### 11. Pricing rules are changing
**Pattern.** "What changes for you" explainers around market-design reforms:
- 15-minute pricing (varttisähkö) from 1 October 2025: 96 prices a day.
- Smart-meter replacement, to be completed by the end of 2028.
- 15-minute netting for solar owners.
- The proposed 5-minute pricing: "Pörssisähköön kaavaillaan viiden minuutin hinnoittelua" (Verkkouutiset/Montel, late 2025), meaning 288 prices a day.

**Examples**
- MTV Uutiset, 2 Apr 2025: "Pörssisähkö siirtyy varttihinnoitteluun – kaksi miljoonaa sähkömittaria vaihtoon" – https://www.mtvuutiset.fi/artikkeli/porssisahko-siirtyy-varttihinnoitteluun-kaksi-miljoonaa-sahkomittaria-vaihtoon/9127214

**The underlying argument.** As price intervals shrink, timing consumption by hand stops being realistic. That pushes the coverage toward automation (angle 15).

### Family D — The total bill

#### 12. Your bill rises without the electricity price rising
**Pattern.** Grid fees (siirtomaksut), new peak-power charges (tehomaksu), taxes and VAT. Yle dominates this angle (16 of 26 indexed articles), with local papers covering their own grid company.

**Sub-angles**
- **Annual distribution price notices.** For example, Caruna's +4.4% in Espoo from 1 January 2026 (Espoon Sanomat, 12 Nov 2025). Yle reported on 19 October 2026 that transmission prices could rise by up to 80% in places *(via Gemini)*.
- **Peak-power charges.** "Sähkön tehomaksu määräytyy jatkossa 15 minuutin huipputehon mukaan – vartin kulutuspiikki voi maksaa kymppejä" (Yle, 10 Oct 2025): the peak-power charge will be based on the highest 15-minute peak, so a single quarter-hour spike can cost tens of euros – https://yle.fi/a/74-20187723. Also: "Mökin pikalämmityksestä saattaa tulevaisuudessa tulla iso sähkönsiirtolasku" (quick-heating the summer cottage may bring a big distribution bill; Yle, 23 Feb 2025) – https://yle.fi/a/74-20144684
- **Underground cabling costs.** "Siirtomaksu voi nousta jopa tonnilla" (the distribution fee could rise by up to €1,000; Yle, 16 Aug 2026) – https://yle.fi/a/74-20241188
- **Regulation and court battles.** Grid companies vs the Energy Authority (Energiavirasto) over how network assets are valued. The Market Court sided with the authority (Espoon Sanomat, 19 Aug 2026, per Gemini), and the companies took the dispute further: "Sähköverkkoyhtiöt valittavat valvonnan muutoksista korkeimpaan hallinto-oikeuteen" (grid companies appeal to the Supreme Administrative Court; Karjalainen, 24 Sep 2026) – https://www.karjalainen.fi/uutissuomalainen/9819956
- **Ownership.** The Caruna sale to Iberdrola: "Näkyykö Carunan omistajavaihdos sähkölaskussa?" (will Caruna's ownership change show in the bill?; MTV, 22 Jul 2026) – https://www.mtvuutiset.fi/artikkeli/nakyyko-carunan-omistajavaihdos-sahkolaskussa-helpottava-arvio/9368938
- **The fiscal wedge.** VAT rose to 25.5% from September 2024, the security-of-supply fee was raised in 2026 *(Gemini; unverified)*, and VAT is charged on top of the electricity tax.

**Why it matters editorially.** This is the most under-covered part of the bill relative to its weight. In summer, when spot averages about 2 c, grid fees and taxes still keep the total unit price above 10 c/kWh *(Gemini's model)*. Households cannot avoid these costs by timing their consumption.

### Family E — Everyday consequences

#### 13. A real household or business struggles
**Pattern.** A named person, local angle, a painful bill and a lifestyle change. Almost entirely concentrated in the January–February 2026 spikes (12 of 13 indexed items fall in 2026 Q1).

**Examples**

## 7. Structural conclusions

1. **Attention is skewed toward the day-ahead auction.** Most visible consumer headlines are about tomorrow's price, a single reactor or futures, while the parts of the bill households can't influence (grid fees, peak charges, taxes) move through regulatory channels with much less coverage. Weather explains more of the price than the outage stories imply.

2. **Households are reaching the limit of how much price risk they can be handed.** The sequence of hourly spot, then hybrid contracts, then 96 quarter-hour prices, then a proposed 288 five-minute prices keeps pushing risk and effort onto households. The resulting fatigue shows up in contract-switching swings, hybrid backlash and the growth of automation stories.

3. **The structural debate is a coordination deadlock, not a scarcity story.** Producers say prices are too low to build; consumer advocates warn data centres will make winter more expensive; grid companies want higher allowed returns. Coverage is moving from "what does tomorrow cost" toward "who pays for the system", and this will intensify ahead of the 2027 parliamentary election.

4. **Every price extreme generates the full set of angles.** One cold, calm week produces alerts, explainers, cost calculators, human-interest pieces, contract advice and political comment within about ten days. A content calendar can be built around that sequence.

## 8. Emerging and minor angles to watch

- **The end of negative prices** as flexibility (batteries, electric boilers) puts a floor under the price.
- **Market-abuse enforcement.** The April 2026 case of a company that crashed prices below zero, now facing a penalty demand.
- **Balancing-market extremes and consumption records** (Fingrid) appearing in mainstream explainers.
- **Is the "golden age" of spot contracts over?** A framing combining data centres, hydro and investment needs.
- **Comparison-site and affiliate content acting as news.** Price trackers and comparison services supply many of the figures and press releases that become stories, and compete with the media for the same searches.

## 9. Data conflicts and caveats

- **Peak price on 3 Feb 2026.** Claude's research has about 77 c/kWh (tracker sites); Gemini has 82.19 c. The daily average of about 48 c is consistent between sources. Verify against Nord Pool.
- **Figures only Gemini reports:** OL3 outage lengths in 2024–2025, Oomi's 67% / +1.15 c hybrid figure, the hydro deficit of about 12 TWh, the security-of-supply fee increase, "47 of 77 grid companies raising fees", the 145 GW / 100 GW Fingrid inquiries, Kiinteistöliitto's €390 estimate, and the Väre OL3 study. These are plausible and partly corroborated by headlines in the index, but should be checked at source before publication.
- **Material outside the window** (January 2024 price record, the 2024 hybrid backlash, the 365 Hankinta case) is used only as background.
- **Paywalls.** HS, Kauppalehti, IS and Uutissuomalainen articles were often coded from headline and lead only.
- **Counts are a search-index sample, not a census.** For true volumes, use a media-monitoring service (Meltwater, M-Brain/Kantar or ePressi Monitor) with deduplication for syndicated text.
